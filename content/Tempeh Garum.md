## Background

The objective is to produce a [[Garum|garum]] from [[Tempeh|tempeh]] to leverage umami synergy. Synergistic umami relies on pairing free glutamates with nucleotides such as IMP and GMP, which are typically concentrated in animal products, yeast, and [[Shiitake|shiitake mushrooms]]. 

During tempeh fermentation, the mold produces small amounts of IMP and GMP. When combined with the enzymatic breakdown from [[Koji|koji]] (releasing free amino acids/glutamates), this should theoretically yield significantly more umami depth than using raw or cooked grains alone.

## Formula

### Ingredients
- 1,636 g Wheat and oat [[Tempeh|tempeh]]
- 1,636 g [[Rye Koji|rye koji]]
- 3,272 g Water (1:1 ratio to total solid substrate)
- 5% Salt by total mass of the mixture (solids + water)

## Notes and Observations

- **Incubation:** Held for 1 month at 60°C.
- **Tasting Notes:** Richer profile than a standard wheat or grain [[Shoyu|shoyu]]. While not quite as heavy or deep as a soy-based amino sauce, it has a distinct, complementary richness with more body. Carries a touch more funk, but remains very approachable. It could make an effective blending component with soy-based ferments.

## Future Adjustments

- Tempeh mold (*Rhizopus*) may not be the most efficient nucleotide producer for this application. Research suggests certain *Placidia mycetes* species yield significantly higher levels of IMP and GMP; running a subsequent trial with those cultures could amplify the umami effect substantially.
