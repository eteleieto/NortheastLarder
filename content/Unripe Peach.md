title: Unripe Peach
aliases: [green peach, unripe peaches]

*Prunus persica*, harvested before ordinary eating ripeness

An unripe peach is a developmental stage rather than a separate variety. The flesh is firm, sour, and only faintly peach-scented because much of the sugar accumulation, softening, and volatile aroma development associated with ripeness has not happened yet.[^1]


Peach is a climacteric fruit: ethylene rises as the fruit ripens and helps coordinate softening. Cell-wall pectins break down, acidity generally falls, and lactones become a larger part of the recognizable peach aroma.[^1]


Peaches can be grown in Maine, but winter cold and spring frost make them less dependable than [[Apple|apples]] or sour cherries. Redhaven and other relatively hardy cultivars are familiar in Northeast orchards; “unripe,” however, describes when the fruit was picked, not which cultivar it came from.[^2]


Unripe [[Unripe Plum|plums]] and peaches share the firm, high-acid character of immature stone fruit. Slightly underripe fruit also tends to contain more functional pectin than overripe fruit, although the amount varies by species and cultivar.[^3]

The drying, puckering sensation of an unripe peach is astringency rather than sourness. Tannins bind saliva proteins together, reducing lubrication and increasing friction in the mouth; ripening weakens that defense as the fruit becomes attractive to seed-dispersing animals.[^4]
