*Matricaria chamomilla* and *Chamaemelum nobile*

“Chamomile” commonly refers to German chamomile or Roman chamomile. The flower heads are used for a floral, apple-like infusion and can also perfume custards, cream, syrups, and baked goods.[^1]

### Kitchen notes

- Use the flowers, not an unspecified plant sold only as “chamomile,” and steep gently to avoid excessive bitterness.
- Dry flowers in a single layer and store airtight, away from light, once fully crisp.[^2]
- The whole plant was originally used as part of preparations of gruit, and so imparts some spice-like notes as well as a fair bit of bitterness.

### Northeast notes

Chamomile is among the commonly grown herbs listed by Penn State for home herb gardens. It can be treated as a small, useful infusion herb rather than a bulk crop.[^1]
