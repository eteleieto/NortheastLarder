*Helianthus tuberosus*

Jerusalem artichoke is a sunflower-family perennial grown for its knobbly underground tubers. It is native to North America and has a long Northeast food history.[^1]


It is more closely related to [[Sunflower Seed|sunflower]] than to the globe artichoke. In some Indigenous “Seven Sisters” accounts from the Northeast, it appears alongside corn, beans, squash, sunflower, and other regional crops.[^1]


Neither half of the English name is botanically helpful: the plant is native to North America rather than Jerusalem, and the food part is a tuber rather than the flower bud eaten from a globe artichoke.[^2]

Unlike a potato, the tuber stores little starch. Its principal reserve is inulin, a long chain of fructose units; human digestive enzymes do not break those chains down efficiently, so intestinal microbes do instead, which can make a large portion uncomfortable.[^3][^4] Long, low cooking can break much of the inulin into digestible fructose, producing a markedly sweeter, softer, translucent-brown result.[^4]

The plant is a tall perennial sunflower that can spread aggressively from leftover tubers, which is part of the reason Native Plant Trust treats it as both a native food plant and a potentially weedy garden subject.[^5] In New England it occurs in all six states, especially in fields, stream banks, disturbed ground, and old cultivation sites. The edible part is an underground storage stem rather than a true root, and its irregular knobs bruise and dehydrate more easily than a smooth potato.[^6]

That storage chemistry gives the tuber a different seasonal rhythm from a potato. The inulin remains a reserve carbohydrate in the ground, and cold conditions can shift the balance toward sweeter flavor; keeping the crop in the soil until needed is therefore practical where the ground stays workable. The same inulin that makes the tuber interesting as a low-starch crop also explains its reputation for digestive discomfort, so it is a food with a distinctive structural carbohydrate rather than a direct potato substitute.[^3][^6]
