*Cucurbita* spp.

Squash blossoms are the edible flowers of the same *Cucurbita* plants that give summer squash, pumpkins, and winter squash.[^1] They are soft, lightly floral, and neutral enough to take on richer fillings or batters without disappearing entirely, which is why they work as more than just a garnish.[^2]

Both male and female blossoms can be eaten, but the distinction matters if the goal is still to harvest squash. Female flowers carry a small swollen ovary behind the bloom, the beginning of the fruit itself, while male flowers only carry pollen and are usually the better ones to pick in quantity.[^1][^2]

That anatomy is also part of the ingredient's appeal. The blossom is delicate and short-lived, but it comes attached to a very productive crop and makes a useful second harvest from the same planting. In practice it is less a novelty flower than a brief seasonal vegetable with a built-in reminder that cucurbits offer edible leaves, flowers, seeds, and fruit rather than just one market form.[^1][^2]
