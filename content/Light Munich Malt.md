*Hordeum vulgare*

Light Munich malt is a more deeply kilned barley malt that still behaves like a base malt. It brings more color and a fuller toasted-grain character than very pale brewing malts, but it still carries enough enzymatic strength to convert starches in a mash, which is exactly what makes it interesting outside beer as well.[^1][^2]

The flavor shift comes from the way malt is finished. Once barley has been germinated to build enzymes and accessible sugars, kilning fixes that balance while also pushing browning reactions and aroma development.[^2] Valley Malt describes its Light Munich as a rich base malt with a ruby-red cast and sweet, aromatic, nutty brown-sugar notes, which tracks with why it works so well in experiments that want both amylase activity and a darker, more cooked flavor.[^1]

For Northeast Larder, the regional piece matters as much as the flavor. Valley Malt grows this malt stream from grain raised in New Hampshire, New York, and Connecticut, so the ingredient is not just "brewing malt" in the abstract but a specific Northeast-processed grain product with real local identity.[^1][^3]
