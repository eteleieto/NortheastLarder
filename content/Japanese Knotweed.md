*Fallopia japonica*

Japanese knotweed is an aggressively invasive perennial whose youngest spring shoots can be cooked as a tart vegetable, in ways similar to rhubarb.[^1]

### Useful facts

- Harvest only young, tender shoots, and only from a stand already being actively controlled.[^1]
- Never plant, transplant, compost, or casually discard knotweed. Even small rhizome fragments can resprout and establish new plants.[^2]

### Northeast notes

Knotweed is widespread and difficult to control in Maine. UMaine recommends repeated cutting or other planned management; culinary harvest can fit into removal, not cultivation.[^1][^2]
