*Levisticum officinale*

Lovage is a stout perennial in the carrot family with a concentrated celery-like aroma. It is native to southwestern Asia and perhaps the Mediterranean, then spread through Europe.[^1]

The leaf is the familiar seasoning, but older European use treated the plant more broadly: the stalks were blanched, boiled, or braised like celery and could also be candied like angelica. That history helps explain both the plant's celery association and the strength needed to use it sparingly.[^1]

[^1]: John Vaughan and Catherine Geissler, *The New Oxford Book of Food Plants*, 2nd ed. (Oxford University Press, 2009), p. 166 (PDF p. 201).
