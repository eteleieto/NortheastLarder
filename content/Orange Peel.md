Orange peel is two ingredients held together. The colored outer epidermis contains glands of aromatic oil, while the white, spongy albedo beneath is rich in pectin and protective bitter phenolics.[^1]

The bitter compounds are water-soluble but the fragrant oils are not. Repeated cold soaking or brief changes of hot water can therefore leach bitterness while retaining much of the oil; gentle cooking then softens the pectin-rich albedo before sugar is introduced for candying.[^1] That same separation helps explain why zest, whole peel, and blanched rind behave so differently in sugar, salt cures, vinegar, [[Cheong|cheong]], kosho, and shio koji.

[^1]: Harold McGee, *On Food and Cooking: The Science and Lore of the Kitchen*, rev. ed. (Scribner, 2004), PDF p. 387.
