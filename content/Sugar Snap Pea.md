*Pisum sativum*

Sugar snap peas are edible-pod peas bred for a thick, crisp pod that can be eaten while the seeds inside are nearly mature. They sit between shelling peas and snow peas: the pod is fuller and crunchier than a snow pea, while the young seeds are still sweet and tender.[^1]

Peas are especially well suited to the Northeast’s cool spring. University of Minnesota Extension gives 55–65°F as a good growing range and recommends planting as soon as the soil is workable; sugar snaps can then be harvested when the pod is filled but still juicy. The vines, tender shoot tips, and tendrils are edible too, giving the plant more food value than the familiar pod alone.[^1]

Their sweetness is fleeting. Once picked, peas lose moisture and sugars quickly, and over-mature pods become starchy with tougher skins.[^2] That short window is part of what makes sugar snaps useful in a regional food system: they reward eating close to harvest and preserve a clear sense of early-summer freshness in preparations such as the notebook’s lacto-fermented snap peas.

[^1]: University of Minnesota Extension, “[Growing peas in home gardens](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peas).”
[^2]: Harold McGee, *On Food and Cooking: The Science and Lore of the Kitchen*, rev. ed. (Scribner, 2004), PDF p. 350.
