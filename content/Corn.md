*Zea mays*

Corn is a grass grown for large, starchy kernels borne on a cob. In the Northeast it spans fresh [[Sweet Corn|sweet corn]], dry grain, nixtamalized preparations, and historically much older Indigenous food systems.[^1][^2]

University of Maine Extension describes sweet corn as one of Maine's late-summer staples, widely sold through local farm stands and markets.[^1] In colder parts of the region, variety choice matters because corn is a warm-season crop and earlier cultivars are more dependable before frost.[^3]

The kernel changes character depending on when it is harvested. Sweet corn is picked while sugars are still high; dry corn is left to harden and becomes useful for milling, roasting, and long storage. That split helps explain why one plant can lead to fresh ears, cornmeal, grits, masa-style preparations, and toasted grain drinks.[^1][^4]

Dry corn types also differ in the architecture of their endosperm. Flint and popcorn have a large proportion of hard, protein-rich endosperm; flour corn is soft and easily ground; dent corn combines hard and soft regions; and sweet corn stores more sugar and less starch while immature. Blue, purple, and red kernels carry water-soluble anthocyanins in the aleurone layer just beneath the hull.[^5]

Traditional alkaline cooking, or nixtamalization, softens the tough hull by dissolving cell-wall hemicellulose. It also helps the ground kernels form a cohesive dough and releases bound niacin in a form the body can absorb.[^5] That one treatment is a good reminder that corn is not just one ingredient but a family of ingredients whose behavior changes radically with harvest stage, kernel type, and processing.

For Northeast cooking, that range matters as much as the crop's history. Sweet corn is the obvious seasonal form, but regional grain projects also lean on older dry-corn traits: dense flint or flour kernels for meal, roasted corn notes, pigment, and long storage.[^2][^5] The ingredient can move from milky and grassy to nutty, chalky, tannic, or deeply toasted without ever leaving the same species.
