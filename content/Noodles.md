## Background

Noodles can be broadly categorized into three production styles based on dough and forming technique:

1. **Shaped and Sheeted Noodles**
   Made from relatively low-hydration doughs (often around 30–50% hydration depending on grain type and flour ash content), which are rolled, laminated, and cut or formed into shape.
   - **[[Wheat]]:** The standard baseline due to glutenin and gliadin forming an extensible, elastic gluten network via kneading or [[autolysis]].
   - **[[Rye]]:** Contains gluten-related storage proteins (secalins), but unlike wheat it does not readily produce a continuous, elastic gluten network. Much of rye dough's cohesion and viscosity instead comes from highly water-binding arabinoxylans and other pentosans.
   - **[[Buckwheat]]:** Completely gluten-free. Cohesion relies on structural amylopectin content and water-soluble mucilage with hydrocolloid properties. Cooked buckwheat combines nutty pyrazines with smoky, green, and faintly fishy notes.[^1] 100% buckwheat doughs ([[Juwari Soba]]) are brittle and fragile; salt is often omitted because it interferes with mucilage hydration.
   - **Hydrocolloid and Starch Modification:** Brittle doughs can be stabilized by introducing external hydrocolloids (e.g., [[Elm Bark|elm bark]] polysaccharides, [[Irish Moss|Irish sea moss]]) or through partial starch gelatinization (scalding a portion of the flour with hot water). [[Alkaline Noodles|Alkalization]] similarly enhances water uptake and swelling of starch granules, increasing firmness and bite.
   - **Egg Additions:** Fat and protein from whole eggs disrupt gluten networks to tenderize the dough, while coagulated egg proteins provide springiness.

2. **Extruded Noodles**
   Formed by forcing dough through a die under pressure.
   - High-pressure extrusion of wheat or semolina doughs preserves a developed protein-starch matrix.
   - Extrusion of gluten-free starch doughs relies on a gelatinized starch binder, often extruding directly into boiling water to set the structure immediately.

3. **Batter-Based Noodles**
   Formed from high-hydration, pourable batters. Common examples include refined starch slurries (such as [[Liangpi]] or [[Rice Noodle|rice noodles]]) and whole-grain batters dropped or scraped into water (such as [[Spätzle]]).

## Notes and Observations

### Test 1: No Salt (Hydration ~50%)

- **80:20 Buckwheat : Wheat**
  - Result: Good flavor, slightly brittle.
  - Notes: Performs better in wider/thicker noodle shapes where bite is preserved. Needs a higher wheat percentage or structural binder for finer cuts.
- **80:20 Rye : Wheat**
  - Result: Held shape well in a thicker cut without noticeable brittleness.
  - Notes: Flavor was surprisingly neutral.
- **50:50 [[Corn]] : Wheat**
  - Result: Texture decent but soft; corn flavor felt muted.
  - Notes: Toasting the cornmeal prior to hydration could improve aroma and compensate for the lower structural contribution.
- **100% Wheat**
  - Result: Overly soft.
  - Notes: Standard all-purpose or unsifted whole wheat flours have bran fibers that shear gluten strands. High-protein, finely bolted flour is necessary for low-hydration sheeting.

### Test 2: 1% Salt Added

- **70:30 Buckwheat : Wheat (50% Hydration)**
  - Result: Cut to a thin soba gauge; texture was too soft and delicate for hot broth.
  - Notes: Better suited to cold applications with a cold-water rinse. Future trials require hot-water scalding or alkalization to improve chew.
- **75:25 Rye : Wheat**
  - Result: Acceptable structure, slightly soft bite.
- **75:25 Rye : Wheat (Scalded Porridge Method)**
  - Process: Pre-gelatinized 1/3 of the rye flour as a hot porridge before mixing.
  - Result: Overly soft and gummy texture.
- **75:25 Rye : Wheat (Alkalized with 1.5% Baking Soda)**
  - Result: Shifted to a vivid yellow hue. Texture improved significantly—much firmer, springier, and bouncier, resembling an alkaline ramen noodle.
  - Notes: Flavor was distinctively alkaline; 1.5% baking soda was slightly aggressive. Scale back to 1.0% in future batches.

### Flour Notes: [[Brooklyn Granary]] Glenn Pasta Flour
- Absorbs less water than standard whole wheat flour.
- Optimal hydration sits around 38–40%.
- Yields a dark finished noodle with a clean, mild whole-wheat flavor.
