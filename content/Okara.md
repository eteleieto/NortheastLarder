---
title: Okara
aliases: [soy pulp, tofu dregs]
tags: [INGREDIENT]
---

Okara is the damp pulp left after ground [[Soybean 1|soybeans]] are filtered to make soy milk or tofu. It is mostly the insoluble part of the bean, so it carries fiber, some protein and fat, and a mild cooked-soy flavor.[^1]


Okara is not soybean meal. Soybean meal is usually the material left after extracting oil, while okara comes from extracting the water-soluble portion during soy-milk production. The two byproducts begin with the same bean but have different moisture, texture, and composition.[^1]


The Japanese word *okara* has been documented in English since the late nineteenth century. A USDA publication included “soy pulp” recipes by the 1930s, although the ingredient remained far more familiar in East Asian tofu-making households and shops.[^2]


Okara sits beside seed presscakes as a second-life ingredient: both are what remains after one fraction has been drawn out. The difference is water extraction here versus oil extraction in [[Sunflower Seed 1|sunflower]] or [[Pumpkin Seed 1|pumpkin seed]] presscake.

[^2]: William Shurtleff and Akiko Aoyagi, “[History of Soybean Fiber Products: Okara and Soy Bran](https://www.soyinfocenter.com/HSS/soybean_fiber_products.php),” Soyinfo Center, 2004.
