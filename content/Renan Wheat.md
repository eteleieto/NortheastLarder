*Triticum aestivum*

Renan is a French winter [[Wheat|bread wheat]] variety created by AgriObtentions, a subsidiary of INRAE, and registered in the early 1990s. Three decades later it is still widely grown, especially in organic farming, prized for yield regularity, disease resistance, and baking value.[^1] Like other modern bread wheats it is hexaploid, carrying six chromosome sets inherited from three grass ancestors, with a genome of roughly 15 billion base pairs.[^1]

Its flour is prized for a subtly sweet aroma and a delicate, buttery crumb — Unified Mills describes Renan bread as tasting buttered even when it isn't — which suits naturally leavened loaves and French-style baking.[^2] In the Northeast it has found a home with regional grain projects: Unified Mills sells a bread flour made from Renan grown organically in New York's Finger Lakes and milled in small batches at the Birkett Mills, a family-run mill in upstate New York operating since the late eighteenth century.[^2]

In this notebook Renan appears as a koji substrate rather than a bread flour. Pearled Renan wheat grew strong mold growth in the grain-substrate trials in [[Koji]], and a three-day cure in Renan shio koji made the tofu in [[Shio Koji Tofu|shio koji tofu]] intensely sour, salty, and soft.
