*Fagopyrum esculentum*

Buckwheat tea, often sold as sobacha, is an infusion of roasted buckwheat groats. It has a toasted, cereal-like, nutty flavor and is naturally caffeine-free because it is not made from the tea plant.


- Brew roasted groats with hot water, then strain; a second infusion is usually worthwhile.
- Use it hot, chilled, or as a subtle cereal note in syrups, custards, and ice creams.


Buckwheat is grown in Maine for grain as well as cover-crop use. It is fast-growing and well adapted to poorer soils; UMaine lists common buckwheat, Lifago, and VNS among varieties grown in the state.[^1]


[^1]: University of Maine Cooperative Extension, “[Maine Forage Facts: Small Grains Crops](https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/2270e/),” accessed July 17, 2026.
