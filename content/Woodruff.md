*Galium odoratum*

Sweet woodruff is a low, shade-tolerant perennial with whorled leaves and small white flowers. Its characteristic hay-and-vanilla aroma becomes more apparent as the leaves wilt or dry.[^1]


The aroma is associated with coumarin, the same broad flavor compound family that gives tonka bean and fresh-cut hay some of their character. This is a small-quantity flavoring plant, not a bulk herb.[^1]


Sweet woodruff spreads by rhizomes and can form a continuous woodland groundcover. In Germany it is strongly associated with *Waldmeister* flavoring.[^2]

Fresh leaves are comparatively quiet; the hay, almond, and vanilla character develops as the plant wilts or dries. That makes woodruff unusual among herbs in the Northeast: its most recognizable food aroma is created after harvest, and a small amount can perfume an infusion without behaving like a conventional leafy seasoning.[^2][^3]

The plant prefers shade and can spread vigorously once established. It is therefore better understood as a cultivated woodland herb than as a casual roadside forage, especially because correct identification matters for any plant gathered for food.[^3][^4]
