*Tilia* spp.

Linden flowers have a delicate honey-like fragrance and are used fresh or dried for a gentle floral tea. Young leaves can also be eaten raw or cooked as a salad green.[^1][^2]

### Kitchen notes

- Dry the flowers promptly, then steep them on their own or blend them with more assertive herbs.
- Use the young leaves early, before they become coarse.

### Northeast notes

American basswood, *Tilia americana*, is a native North American linden. Linden trees are a fitting source for a very light, local summer tisane, but harvest only from unsprayed trees away from roads.[^2]
