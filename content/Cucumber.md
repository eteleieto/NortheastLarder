*Cucumis sativus*

Cucumber is clean, watery, and crisp, with a mild green flavor. It is a warm-season cucurbit whose high water content makes it feel especially refreshing.[^1]


Cucumber is a cucurbit selected for watery, crisp fruit rather than the dense flesh and long storage of a winter squash.


Maine growers distinguish pickling types from slicing types partly by skin thickness and water content; those traits affect texture after preservation.[^2]

“Pickled” does not necessarily mean fermented. A cucumber can be acidified directly with vinegar, kept refrigerated in an acid brine, or fully fermented by lactic-acid bacteria; the three routes produce different flavors, textures, and keeping qualities.[^3]

The best cucumber for a crisp pickle is not necessarily the best cucumber for a salad. Pickling types tend to have thicker skins, smaller seeds, and less watery flesh, while slicing types are bred for a tender, juicy bite.[^4] Crispness is also a race against time: Maine Extension recommends refrigerating cucumbers promptly and using them soon after harvest. The blossom end contains enzymes that can soften pickles, while calcium helps maintain the fruit’s natural pectin structure during preservation.[^4]
