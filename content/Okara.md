---
title: Okara
aliases: [soy pulp, tofu dregs]
tags: [INGREDIENT]
---

Okara is the damp pulp left after ground [[Soybean|soybeans]] are filtered to make soy milk or tofu. It is mostly the insoluble part of the bean, so it carries fiber, some protein and fat, and a mild cooked-soy flavor.[^1]


Okara is not soybean meal. Soybean meal is usually the material left after extracting oil, while okara comes from extracting the water-soluble portion during soy-milk production. The two byproducts begin with the same bean but have different moisture, texture, and composition.[^1] Because okara starts as a wet residue rather than a dry presscake, it behaves more like a fresh mash than a shelf-stable pantry ingredient.


The ingredient has a long second life in fermented foods rather than only in frugal home cooking. Steinkraus describes soybean-milk or tofu residue as a workable substrate for tempe-like products and notes tempe gembus, an Indonesian okara tempe, as one established example.[^2][^3] That history matters here because okara’s value is structural as much as nutritional: once the soluble soy milk has been pulled away, the remaining particles can still be knit together, dried, browned, or fermented into something with more bite than a puree.


The Japanese word *okara* has been documented in English since the late nineteenth century. A USDA publication included “soy pulp” recipes by the 1930s, although the ingredient remained far more familiar in East Asian tofu-making households and shops.[^4]

Okara sits beside seed presscakes as a second-life ingredient: both are what remains after one fraction has been drawn out. The difference is water extraction here versus oil extraction in [[Sunflower Seed|sunflower]] or [[Pumpkin Seed|pumpkin seed]] presscake. That makes okara especially relevant to a notebook already interested in byproducts, because it is less a waste product than a partially transformed bean waiting for a second process.
