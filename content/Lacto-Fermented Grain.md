## Background

Testing the effects of [[Lacto-Fermentation|lacto-fermentation]] on whole and processed grains, comparing standard lacto-fermentations against nixtamalized versions and screening various grain substrates. [[Barley]] previously developed complex, cheesy flavors under similar conditions, prompting further exploration across rye, wheat, malt, and oats to see how starch and grain composition affect final flavor and acidity.

## Process

### Ingredients
* Pearled [[Rye|rye]] (or other test grains: cracked [[Wheat|wheat]], [[Malt|malt]], [[Rolled Oats|rolled oats]])
* Water (equal parts by weight to grain)
* Salt (4% of total weight)
* [[Apple Cider Vinegar]] (for nixtamalized grain trials: 6% of grain weight)
* Optional starter brine: Active brine from a prior vegetable lacto-ferment for back-slopping

### Method

1. **Standard Grain Ferment:** Combine equal parts grain and water with 4% salt by total weight. 
2. **Nixtamalized Trial:** Combine equal parts nixtamalized grain and water with 4% salt. Add apple cider vinegar (6% of grain weight) to rapidly lower the initial pH, helping lactic acid bacteria outcompete undesirable microbes.
3. **Acidification & Safety Note:** When fermenting high-starch grain substrates, early acidification is critical to suppress potential pathogens like [[Bacillus Cereus|*Bacillus cereus*]]. While spontaneous fermentation can work, back-slopping with active starter brine from a vegetable ferment provides a sufficient microbial load to safely colonize the substrate quickly.

## Notes and Observations

### Trial 1: Pearled Rye vs. Nixtamalized Rye
* **Nixtamalized Rye:** Developed a distinct sulfurous profile closely reminiscent of hard-cooked egg.
* **Standard Rye:** Clean, lactic, and grainy. While it lacked the pronounced cheese-like profile seen in barley, it carried a pleasant, mild funk with solid culinary utility.
* **Ferment Liquid Yield:** The strained brine from both trials was intensely flavorful—well-suited for vinaigrettes, seasoned mayonnaises, or blending into [[Miso|miso]].
* **Fermented Solids:** The softened grains show potential as bread dough inclusions to add acidity and fermented character, or blended smooth into savory grain creams.

### Trial 2: Substrate Screenings
* **Cracked Wheat (3 days room temp + 3 days cold hold):** Brine tasted very similar to fresh mozzarella liquid, though slightly more sour and saline, finishing with a distinct cheesy aftertaste.
* **Malt:** Developed a pronounced sour beer profile—savory, sharp, and complex.
* **Rolled Oats:** Sour and tangy with a sharp, goat-cheese-like character; less rounded than the cracked wheat.
* **Fermented Rye:** Profile landed close to the oats, with a mild, underlying rye earthiness.

## Future Adjustments

* **Co-Fermentations:** Test hybrid batches combining mixed grains or grain-and-legume blends.
* **Starter Ratios:** Standardize back-slop percentages using mature lacto-brines to establish reliable acidification timelines across high-starch substrates.
