*Asparagus officinalis*

Asparagus is a perennial vegetable harvested for its young shoots. It originated in Asia Minor and has been cultivated for more than 2,000 years; in Maine, it is one of the first major spring vegetables.[^1]


The edible spear is a rapidly growing shoot. If left alone, it develops into a tall, fern-like plant that replenishes the crown for the following year.[^1]


Unlike an annual vegetable, a well-kept asparagus bed is a long-lived planting. The first seasons are an investment in the crown; established plants can then return for years.[^1]

Freshness matters unusually much after harvest. The spear remains a rapidly growing shoot and continues consuming its stored sugars faster than any other common vegetable; sweetness and juiciness fall while fiber develops, especially during the first day and when the spears are kept warm or exposed to light.[^2]

Some of that toughness cannot be cooked away. Thick-walled, lignified supporting cells persist through heat and create the stringy texture of older spears, while harvest and storage stresses can accelerate further lignification.[^3]
