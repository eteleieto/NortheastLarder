---
title: Cauliflower
tags: [INGREDIENT]
---

*Brassica oleracea* var. *botrytis*

Cauliflower is a mild, dense brassica grown for its compact white curd: proliferating, undeveloped flowering tissue that has stopped before forming an ordinary loose flower head.[^1]

The curd is not a single flower but a tightly packed mass of immature flower stalks and buds. White cultivars are kept from sunlight so chlorophyll does not turn the curd green; purple, green, and yellow cultivars let those pigments show, and Romanesco arranges the same flowering growth into repeating pointed spirals.[^2]

Cauliflower is a cool-season crop that fits the Northeast particularly well as a late crop. Maine guidance lists it for spring and fall plantings, while Penn State recommends late-season plantings so the curd develops before autumn frost.[^3] It is more sensitive to temperature swings than its close relative broccoli: premature reproductive growth can produce small or irregular heads, a problem often called buttoning.[^4]

The entire curd is edible, along with the tender leaves and stalks surrounding it. Its pale color and mild flavor come from the absence of leafy chlorophyll rather than from a different plant family, while its brassica character includes glucosinolates, sulfur-containing defense compounds that can become more noticeable as the vegetable is cut and heated.[^5]
