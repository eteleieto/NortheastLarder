*Ribes rubrum* group

Red currants are translucent, sharply acidic berries carried in dangling clusters. They belong to *Ribes*, the same genus as black currants and gooseberries.


Currants have an unusual legal and ecological history in Maine. *Ribes* plants are part of the life cycle of white pine blister rust, so planting and possession are restricted across many Maine counties and towns.[^1]


The quarantine is not about the fruit itself. It exists because the rust alternates between *Ribes* and five-needle pines, including eastern white pine.[^1]

Cultivated red currants are not a single untouched wild lineage: they developed from several closely related *Ribes* species, including *R. sativum*, *R. petraeum*, and later *R. rubrum*.[^2] The translucent skin encloses numerous seeds in juicy flesh, and the fruit's sharpness reflects a citric-acid content reported around 2.5%.[^2] In New England, garden red currant is recorded as an introduced plant in every state, persisting around roadsides, hedgerows, riverbanks, forest fragments, and abandoned homesteads.[^3]

The fruit is a botanical berry, but the useful harvest arrives as a whole raceme rather than as isolated berries. The flowers open in spring on older wood, and the small fruits color from green to translucent red as they fill with juice.[^2] That growth habit makes currants especially suited to cool-climate gardens, where a shrub can occupy a narrow edge and still produce a vivid crop. The thin skin and high acidity also explain why red currants feel more like a preserving fruit than a hand-to-mouth berry: the flavor is bright and piercing, with enough pectin and acid to give cooked fruit a clean set.[^2]

Maine's currant story is therefore partly a story about what cannot be planted. White pine blister rust needs both a *Ribes* host and a five-needle pine to complete its life cycle, so the state's restrictions vary by county rather than treating every garden situation identically.[^1] The berries may be a traditional small-fruit crop, but a Northeast grower has to check the current quarantine map before treating a red-currant shrub as an ordinary perennial.
