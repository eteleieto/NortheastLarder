*Aspergillus oryzae* and related koji molds

Koji spores, or *tane-koji*, are the inoculum used to begin a fresh batch of [[Koji|koji]]. They are sold either as a concentrated powder or on dried grains of rice or barley; both forms can inoculate a different cooked-grain substrate.[^1][^2]

Commercial tane-koji is not just raw mold dust. Traditional producers select strains for the enzyme profile they want, especially the amylases, proteases, and lipases that make koji useful for starch, protein, and fat breakdown. In dry form the spores keep for months, which is one reason they function so well as a pantry ingredient rather than a strictly perishable culture.[^2]

Once those spores land on a suitable cooked grain, they germinate into hyphae, spread into a white mycelial network, and turn the grain into sweet, fragrant koji over roughly two days.[^3] When using the powdered form, it helps to dilute a very small dose with toasted flour or starch first so it can be sifted evenly across the substrate; otherwise it is easy to overconcentrate one patch and miss another.[^1]
