*Rosa* spp.

Rose hips are the ripe fruits of roses. Their tart, fruity pulp works in tea, jelly, jam, syrup, dried blends, and fruit sauces.[^1]

### Useful facts

- Hips are best collected when fully colored and ripe; they become sweeter as they soften.[^1]
- Remove the stem and blossom ends and strain or remove the seeds before use, as the seed hairs can be irritating.[^1]

### Northeast notes

Several wild roses—including Virginia, Carolina, and shining rose—occur in Maine. UMaine notes that their pulpy hips can be eaten raw or made into jam.[^2]
