*Beta vulgaris*

Beet roots are sweet and earthy, while the leaves have the mineral character of a cooking green. Root colors range from deep red to gold, white, and striped pink.[^1]


Swiss chard is the same species as beet, selected for large leaves and stems rather than an enlarged root.[^2]

Red beet color comes from betalain pigments rather than the anthocyanins responsible for many other red and purple fruits. Yellow beets carry a different balance of the same pigment family.[^3]

Those pigments are water-soluble, so cutting and cooking damage cells and let the color bleed into neighboring ingredients. The earthy aroma is largely geosmin, a compound the beet root itself can produce. Cooked beets also remain unusually firm because phenolic compounds reinforce their cell walls.[^4]
