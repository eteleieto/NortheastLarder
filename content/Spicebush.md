*Lindera benzoin*

Spicebush is a native shrub at the northern edge of its range in Maine. It has aromatic leaves, twigs, bark, and red fall berries; the berries can be eaten fresh or dried and ground as a local spice.[^1]


Spicebush is dioecious: male and female flowers occur on separate plants, so both are needed for berry production.[^1]


The red berries have an allspice-like character, but the shrub offers more than fruit. Young leaves can be eaten, and the twigs and bark are fragrant enough to steep as a tea. A small amount of camphor in the leaves helps explain the plant's warm, resinous aroma, which is present in the leaves, flowers, and bark as well as the berries.[^1]


In Maine it is considered uncommon and favors protected, evenly moist sites, often in an understory or near a brook. The plant is dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate shrubs, so a berry-producing patch needs both sexes. Its presence is therefore more local and particular than that of common garden herbs; harvesting is most meaningful where the shrub is abundant rather than from a lone wild plant.[^1]

[^1]: University of Maine Cooperative Extension, “[Maine Home Garden News — October 2018](https://extension.umaine.edu/gardening/2018/10/01/maine-home-garden-news-october-2018/),” 2018.
