*Zea mays* ‘Bloody Butcher’

Bloody Butcher is an open-pollinated heirloom dent corn with mottled dark-red kernels. It is field corn rather than [[Sweet Corn|sweet corn]]: the mature dry grain is the ingredient, especially after grinding.[^1]

### History

The variety is documented in Appalachia from the nineteenth century and was kept alive through family seed saving. Its startling name is usually explained as a comparison between red-splashed kernels and a butcher’s apron.[^2]

### Plant notes

Dent corn gets the little depression at the crown of each dry kernel because its harder outer starch and softer inner starch shrink differently as the grain dries. Bloody Butcher plants can grow very tall and take around one hundred days or more to mature, which makes them a more demanding fit for the shortest Northeast seasons.[^2]

### Connections

The red color sits mainly in the outer kernel tissues, so milling choices change how much color remains in the meal. [[Nixtamalization]] changes the texture and aroma of the same grain through alkaline treatment rather than through variety alone.
