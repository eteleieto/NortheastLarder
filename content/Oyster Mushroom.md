*Pleurotus* spp.

Oyster mushrooms are wood-decomposing fungi that form layered, shelf-like clusters. The “oyster” name refers to the cap shape, not to a marine relationship.


The mushroom is only the reproductive fruiting body. Most of the organism is a network of hyphae growing through its substrate.[^1]


Oyster mushrooms are among the specialty mushrooms cultivated in Maine, both in small educational projects and commercial mushroom systems.[^1]

Fresh mushrooms are roughly 90% water, which helps explain their short life and dramatic shrinkage. Oyster mushrooms are among the types traditionally dried for storage; lower drying temperatures better preserve color, aroma, texture, and the ability to rehydrate than harsher heat.[^2]

[^1]: University of Maine Cooperative Extension, “[Farm-Scale Mushroom Cultivation](https://extension.umaine.edu/agriculture/2015/10/06/farm-scoop-october-2015/),” 2015.
[^2]: Mohammed Wasim Siddiqui and Asgar Ali, eds., *Postharvest Management of Horticultural Crops* (Apple Academic Press, 2016), PDF p. 192.
