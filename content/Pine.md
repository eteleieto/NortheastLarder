*Pinus* spp.

Pine needles can be used as a resinous aromatic in infusions and vinegars. Their flavor is sharp, woodsy, and slightly citrusy; young needles tend to be less tannic and less bitter than older needles.[^1]

### Kitchen notes

- Chop young, clean needles and steep or infuse them; do not treat them as an interchangeable substitute for every conifer.
- Use a known edible pine species and avoid ornamental or sprayed trees. Positive identification is essential because some conifer look-alikes are unsafe.[^1]

### Northeast notes

Pine is a common Maine forest ingredient, but the practical route is to work with a local, confidently identified species and small quantities rather than harvesting indiscriminately.[^1][^2]
