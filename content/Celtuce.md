*Lactuca sativa* var. *angustana*

Celtuce is a stem lettuce: a lettuce grown mainly for its thick, crisp stalk rather than for a head of leaves.[^1] It is still *Lactuca sativa*, the same species as familiar leaf and head lettuces, but selection has moved the plant’s useful mass into the stem. McGee describes it as especially popular in Asia, where the stalk is stripped, peeled, sliced, and cooked, while the younger leaves remain usable but secondary.[^1] Cornell’s vegetable-variety notes describe the stalk as thick and tender with a celery-like flavor, which explains both the name and the confusion it causes.[^2]

That stalk is the important food part. Fresh celtuce has the watery crunch and faint bitterness you would expect from a lettuce relative, but the peeled stem has more density than salad greens and stays recognizable after cooking or fermentation.[^1][^2] Like other lettuces, it bleeds white latex when cut; McGee points out that even the name *Lactuca* refers to that milky sap.[^1] The leaves can still be eaten, but the plant’s real value is that unusual middle ground between leaf and stalk. The stalk is also where timing matters most: recent breeding notes describe bolting as a limit on the growing window because the stem becomes bitter as the plant moves toward flowering.[^3]

In a Northeastern kitchen, celtuce makes sense less as a canonical regional crop than as a cool-season vegetable that can slot into the same part of the year as lettuce. Cornell’s gardening program lists it with a roughly ninety-day maturity window, which helps explain why it works as a deliberate garden vegetable rather than as an exotic one-off curiosity.[^2] That combination of crisp stem, lettuce bitterness, and enough structure to ferment or juice is exactly why it keeps turning up in experiments here.

The plant’s identity is useful precisely because it refuses the usual lettuce categories. Its leaves are still tender salad greens when young, but the stem is the selected storage organ, thickening above the soil while the plant remains a cool-season annual. Once the stalk starts to elongate toward flowering, the balance shifts from juicy crunch toward bitterness, so celtuce is best understood as a timing-sensitive stem vegetable rather than a lettuce head with an unusual shape.[^2][^3] That makes the peeled stalk the page’s central ingredient: it carries enough water and crisp cell structure to stay recognizable when salted, juiced, or fermented, while the leaves supply a more familiar lettuce character.
